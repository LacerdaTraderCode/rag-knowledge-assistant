# Sleep Hygiene

The circadian rhythm is the body's internal clock, which runs on a cycle of roughly twenty-four hours and is set mainly by light. Bright light soon after waking anchors the clock, while bright screens late at night delay the release of melatonin and push bedtime later. A consistent wake-up time does more for sleep quality than a consistent bedtime.

Caffeine has a half-life of about five to six hours, which means a cup consumed late in the afternoon can still be half as strong when you go to bed. Alcohol makes it easier to fall asleep but fragments the second half of the night and reduces REM sleep, the stage most associated with vivid dreaming and memory consolidation.

A cool, dark bedroom, around 18 degrees Celsius, helps the body lower its core temperature. If you are still awake after about twenty minutes, it is better to get up and do something calm in dim light than to lie there watching the clock.
