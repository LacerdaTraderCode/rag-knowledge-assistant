# Coffee Extraction

Brewing coffee is the act of dissolving flavor compounds out of ground beans into hot water. Acids dissolve first, then sugars, and bitter compounds last, which explains the two classic failures: under-extracted coffee tastes sour and thin, while over-extracted coffee tastes bitter and harsh.

Four variables control the result. Grind size sets how much surface area the water can reach: a finer grind extracts faster, a coarser one more slowly. Water temperature, usually between 92 and 96 degrees Celsius for filter brewing, sets how aggressively compounds dissolve. Brew time is how long water and grounds stay in contact. The brew ratio is the weight of coffee relative to water, and 1:16, or 1 gram of coffee for every 16 grams of water, is a common starting point.

When a cup tastes sour, grind finer or brew longer; when it tastes bitter, go coarser or shorten the brew. Weighing the coffee and the water makes these adjustments repeatable.
