# Sourdough Basics

A sourdough starter is a living mixture of flour and water inhabited by wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria. To keep it healthy, a baker feeds it regularly: discard most of it, then add fresh flour and water. A starter is ready to use when it has roughly doubled in volume and is full of bubbles.

Hydration is the weight of water divided by the weight of flour. A dough at 75 percent hydration is wetter than one at 65 percent, which gives a more open crumb but is stickier and harder to shape. After mixing, the dough goes through bulk fermentation, its first long rise, which can take four to eight hours at room temperature. Warmth speeds fermentation up and cold slows it down, which is why many bakers shape the loaf and then proof it overnight in the refrigerator.

Baking in a preheated Dutch oven traps steam around the loaf, letting it expand fully before the crust hardens.
