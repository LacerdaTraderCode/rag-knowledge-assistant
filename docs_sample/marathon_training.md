# Marathon Training Basics

Most marathon plans run between sixteen and twenty weeks and raise weekly mileage gradually, commonly by no more than about ten percent from one week to the next. The cornerstone of the week is the long run, which grows by a few kilometers at a time and peaks at around thirty to thirty-two kilometers (roughly twenty miles) about three weeks before race day.

Most of the running should be easy, at a pace where you could hold a conversation, with only a minority of the weekly volume spent on tempo runs and intervals. This split, often called the 80/20 approach, builds endurance while limiting injury risk.

In the final two to three weeks, the taper cuts volume substantially while keeping some short, quicker running, so the legs arrive at the start line fresh rather than fatigued. Fueling is trainable as well: long runs are the time to practice taking in thirty to sixty grams of carbohydrate per hour, which delays the sudden exhaustion runners call hitting the wall.
