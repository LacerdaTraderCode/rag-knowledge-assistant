# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae, and some bacteria turn light into chemical energy stored in sugar. In plants it happens inside chloroplasts, organelles filled with the pigment chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light and reflects green, which is why leaves look green.

The process has two stages. In the light-dependent reactions, which take place in the thylakoid membranes, light energy splits water molecules, releasing oxygen as a by-product and producing the energy carriers ATP and NADPH. In the Calvin cycle, which takes place in the fluid-filled stroma, those carriers power the fixation of carbon dioxide into sugar. Summed up, carbon dioxide and water plus light energy become glucose and oxygen.

Leaves take in carbon dioxide through tiny pores called stomata. Opening them also lets water vapor escape, so a plant constantly balances the need for carbon dioxide against the risk of drying out.
